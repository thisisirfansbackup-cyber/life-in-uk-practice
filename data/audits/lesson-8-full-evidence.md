**Checked:** 2026-11-21. **Source:** *Life in the UK: A guide for new residents*, 3rd edition, Crown copyright, `https://storage.googleapis.com/britizen-public/static/study-guide/document.pdf`; relevant passages read from the extracted full text. ## Findings

- Answers 169–192 are supported, with wording tightened and four distinct choices.
- **171:** false; complaints are not limited to writing to the Chief Constable. Direct-to-force and oversight routes exist, subject to eligibility.
- **176:** Scottish and Northern Irish notes are valid currency but not legal tender; shops need not accept them.
- **179 (targeted follow-up, 2026-11-23):** remains select-two: the owner's name and address. The ordinary England/Wales rule puts these on the collar or an attached plate/badge; [Control of Dogs Order 1992, art. 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/901/article/2/made) also specifies exemptions, including working uses and registered guide dogs. It does not substitute identification elsewhere on the dog.
- **185:** restored as a genuine select-two: Saint Helena and the Falkland Islands are British Overseas Territories; the Isle of Man and Jersey are Crown Dependencies.
- **188:** Senedd elections are held every four years from 2026, per the Senedd's current guidance. The older handbook's four-year wording happens to agree, but the citation is the current Senedd page.
- **190:** the prize has been called **The Booker Prize** since 2019; “Man Booker Prize” is the former name. The runtime question uses the current name and the explanation records the former name.
- No unresolved factual blocker in this lesson.
