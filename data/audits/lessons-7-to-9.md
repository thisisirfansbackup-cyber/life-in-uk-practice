# Fact-check: Life in the UK lessons 7–9

**Access date for all sources: 2026-11-07.** The paid official Life in the UK guide was not publicly accessible and was not read. Assessments below fact-check the supplied paraphrases against accessible official public sources; “unconfirmed” means I could not substantiate the exact claim from those sources, not that it is false.

| Lesson/item | Current assessment | Concise evidence / locator (official source) |
|---|---|---|
| 7.1 citizen responsibilities | Confirmed | GOV.UK’s citizenship ceremony guidance lists respecting the rights and freedoms of others and obeying the law; local environmental care is a reasonable civic example, not an exhaustive statutory duty. [S1, “The pledge”] |
| 7.2 Halloween, 31 Oct | Confirmed | Halloween is observed on 31 October. Exact date is supported by the Royal Collection Trust’s calendar entry. [S2, “Halloween”] |
| 7.3 Bill of Rights gave women the vote | **Corrected (false)** | Bill of Rights 1689 concerns parliamentary liberties/succession, not women’s franchise; women’s parliamentary voting rights came much later, in stages (1918, then equal terms in 1928). [S3, ch. 2; S4, “Women’s suffrage”] |
| 7.4 The Ashes | Confirmed | The Ashes is the Test-cricket contest between England and Australia. [S5, “The Ashes”] |
| 7.5 Manufacturing was largest employment source in 18th century | **Unconfirmed / overly broad** | “18th century” covers a century of major economic transition; I found no accessible official source substantiating manufacturing as the single largest employment source across that entire period. Do not teach as an unqualified fact. |
| 7.6 Bannockburn, 1314; Bruce defeated English | **Corrected (overstated)** | Battle of Bannockburn was in 1314 and Robert Bruce’s forces defeated Edward II’s English army. “Scotland remained unconquered” is an absolute/long-term conclusion not established by the battle source. [S6, “Battle of Bannockburn”] |
| 7.7 Smoking where prohibited is criminal offence | Confirmed, with scope | Smoke-free premises rules create offences for smoking in prohibited premises in England; devolved nations have their own legislation. [S7, “Offences and penalties”; S8] |
| 7.8 MacDonalds of Glencoe killed after oath issue | Confirmed, qualified | The 1692 massacre followed a delayed oath of allegiance by MacIain; the official Scottish Parliament history describes the context and massacre. “Because of the oath” alone oversimplifies the political context. [S9, “Glencoe massacre”] |
| 7.9 Devolved areas include health and education | Confirmed, with territorial variation | Health and education are devolved in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, though reserved matters and settlement differ. [S10, “Devolution”] |
| 7.10 Franz Ferdinand assassination led to WWI | Confirmed, shorthand | His assassination on 28 June 1914 was the immediate trigger/crisis leading to war, not the sole underlying cause. [S11, “Causes of the First World War”] |
| 7.11 Scotland capital Edinburgh | Confirmed | Scottish Government identifies Edinburgh as Scotland’s capital. [S12, opening overview] |
| 7.12 1833 Emancipation Act abolished slavery across British Empire | **Corrected (qualified)** | The Slavery Abolition Act 1833 provided for abolition in most of the British Empire, with important territorial exceptions and an apprenticeship transition; abolition took effect in 1834, not uniformly on passage. [S13, ss. 1, 64; S14, “Abolition of slavery”] |
| 7.13 Highland Clearances | Unconfirmed (fragment) | A topic label, not a testable proposition. Official National Records of Scotland material documents evictions/emigration, but no specific claim was supplied. [S15, “Highland and Islands emigration”] |
| 7.14 Falklands are a British Overseas Territory, not part of UK | Confirmed | GOV.UK explicitly labels Falkland Islands a British Overseas Territory; Overseas Territories are constitutionally distinct from the UK. [S16, title/overview; S17, “Overseas Territories”] |
| 7.15 Marston Moor and Naseby were English Civil War battles | Confirmed | UK Parliament’s historical material identifies both among Civil War battles. [S18, “The English Civil War”] |
| 7.16 Suffragettes fought for women’s vote | Confirmed, but not the whole movement | Suffragettes were a militant wing of the women’s suffrage movement; “suffragists” also campaigned by non-militant methods. [S4, “Women’s suffrage”] |
| 7.17 Britain’s favourite view in 2007 was Wastwater/Lake District | Unconfirmed | No official public-institution source located for the poll, its exact wording, or methodology. Do not present as a current fact; it is at most a dated poll result. |
| 7.18 First Stone Age Britons were hunter-gatherers | Confirmed, broad | British Museum’s prehistory material describes early humans as hunter-gatherers; “first Britons” is an imprecise label for prehistoric populations. [S19, “Palaeolithic”] |
| 7.19 Reformation challenged Pope | Confirmed, shorthand | English Reformation severed the English church’s authority from Rome/the Pope; it was a complex political and religious process. [S20, “The Reformation”] |
| 7.20 National Insurance applications contact DWP | **Corrected / unconfirmed as phrased** | National Insurance number applications are handled through GOV.UK/HMRC processes, not generically by contacting DWP; process depends on the applicant’s circumstances. [S21, “Apply for a National Insurance number”] |
| 7.21 Royal Ascot is a major English horse-racing event | Confirmed | Royal Ascot is an annual race meeting in Ascot, Berkshire. [S22, “Royal Ascot”] |
| 7.22 Scottish jury has 15 members | Confirmed for criminal trials | Scottish criminal juries comprise 15 people; this does not apply to every proceeding (civil juries differ). [S23, “Jury service”; S24, “Criminal procedure”] |
| 7.23 Lent is 40 days before Easter | **Corrected (imprecise)** | Lent is the 40-day fasting season leading to Easter, traditionally excluding Sundays in the Western calendar; it is not simply a fixed 40 calendar days immediately before Easter. [S25, “Lent”] |
| 7.24 Last successful foreign invasion of England was 1066 | Confirmed, qualified | The Norman Conquest culminated in William’s victory at Hastings in 1066. “Last successful invasion” depends on defining invasion/conquest and “England”; present as the conventional historical claim. [S26, “Norman Conquest”] |
| 8.1 Germany invaded Poland to start WWII | Confirmed, shorthand | Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939; Britain and France declared war two days later. The invasion began the European war, not every theatre of WWII. [S27, “Second World War”] |
| 8.2 Scotland’s flower is the thistle | Confirmed | National Records of Scotland identifies the thistle as Scotland’s national flower/emblem. [S28, “The thistle”] |
| 8.3 Police complaints only by writing to chief constable | **Corrected (false)** | In England and Wales complaints can be made through the police force or the IOPC; written letter to chief constable is not the only route. Rules differ in Scotland/NI. [S29, “Make a complaint”] |
| 8.4 Home Secretary responsible for crime / policing / immigration | **Corrected (too broad)** | Home Secretary leads the Home Office, whose remits include immigration and crime/justice policy; operational policing is not simply the Home Secretary’s personal responsibility and some policing is devolved. [S30, “About us”; S10] |
| 8.5 St David’s Day, 1 March | Confirmed | Welsh Government observes St David’s Day on 1 March. [S31, “St David’s Day”] |
| 8.6 Mary, Queen of Scots executed after imprisonment | Confirmed | She was imprisoned and executed at Fotheringhay Castle in 1587. [S32, “Mary, Queen of Scots”] |
| 8.7 Francis Crick discovered DNA structure | **Corrected (attribution incomplete)** | Crick and James Watson proposed the DNA double-helix structure, using crucial work/data by Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins; “Crick discovered it” alone is misleading. [S33, “The discovery of DNA”] |
| 8.8 Scottish and Northern Irish banknotes valid everywhere in UK | **Corrected (legal-tender nuance)** | These notes can circulate and are accepted across the UK, but are not legal tender in England and Wales; “valid everywhere” conflates circulation, acceptance and legal tender. [S34, “Banknotes”] |
| 8.9 15 national parks in England/Wales/Scotland | Confirmed (current count) | Official lists show 10 in England, 3 in Wales and 2 in Scotland = 15; excludes Northern Ireland. [S35, list; S36, list; S37, list] |
| 8.10 Northern Ireland part of Great Britain | **Corrected (false)** | Great Britain is England, Scotland and Wales; UK is Great Britain and Northern Ireland. [S38, “Countries of the UK”] |
| 8.11 Dog in public must wear collar with owner name/address | Confirmed, jurisdiction-limited | In England, Control of Dogs Order 1992 requires a dog in a public place to wear a collar with owner’s name and address (or identification on the dog). Do not generalize without checking devolved rules. [S39, art. 2] |
| 8.12 Good Friday commemorates Jesus’s death | Confirmed | Good Friday commemorates the crucifixion/death of Jesus in Christian tradition. [S40, “Good Friday”] |
| 8.13 MP represents everyone in constituency | Confirmed, in representative sense | Each constituency elects an MP to represent it in the House of Commons; MPs may assist constituents regardless of vote, party or voting eligibility. [S41, “Find your MP” / role description] |
| 8.14 Magna Carta restricted King’s power | Confirmed, qualified | Magna Carta set limits on royal authority and affirmed rights for particular groups; not a modern universal rights charter. [S42, “Magna Carta”] |
| 8.15 FGM / taking a person abroad for FGM is illegal | Confirmed | FGM is an offence; arranging/taking a UK national or permanent UK resident abroad for FGM is also criminalised under the 2003 Act. [S43, ss. 1, 4] |
| 8.16 Women first granted UK vote in 1918 | **Corrected (qualification)** | In 1918 some women aged 30+ meeting property/other conditions gained the vote; equal voting terms with men came in 1928. [S44, “Representation of the People Act 1918”; S45] |
| 8.17 St Helena and Falklands are British Overseas Territories | Confirmed | Both appear on the UK Government’s official list of Overseas Territories. [S17, territory list] |
| 8.18 English fleet defeated Spanish Armada in 1588 | Confirmed, simplified | The 1588 Spanish Armada was defeated after English naval action and adverse weather; “English fleet alone defeated it” overstates the causes. [S46, “Spanish Armada”] |
| 8.19 Cenotaph designed by Edwin Lutyens | Confirmed | Historic England lists Sir Edwin Lutyens as architect/designer of the Cenotaph. [S47, listing description] |
| 8.20 Welsh Parliament/Government elected every four years | **Confirmed for current ordinary cycle, with wording qualification** | The 2024 Act changed the ordinary Senedd election interval to four years, effective after the first election held after 7 November 2025; the 2026 Senedd election has occurred. The Welsh Government is formed through the Senedd, not elected in a separate direct election. Statutory exceptions apply. [Senedd Cymru (Members and Elections) Act 2024, ss. 3, 25(3)] |
| 8.21 Beatles rise/social laws liberalized in 1960s | Unconfirmed / causal overclaim | The decade saw cultural and legislative changes, but the claim that Beatles’ rise caused social laws to liberalize is not established by official sources and is too broad. |
| 8.22 Man Booker Prize is for literature | Confirmed, imprecise | The Booker Prize is a literary prize for a novel written in English and published in the UK/Ireland; it is not a general “literature” prize. [S50, “About the Booker Prize”] |
| 8.23 MP death/resignation causes by-election | Confirmed, subject to exceptions | A vacant Commons seat is normally filled by a by-election; exceptions can include a general election being imminent or other parliamentary procedure. [S51, “By-elections”] |
| 8.24 Turner Prize is contemporary art award | Confirmed | Tate describes it as an annual contemporary art prize. [S52, “Turner Prize”] |
| 9.1 Northern Ireland flower shamrock | Confirmed, with terminology caveat | Shamrock is conventionally associated with Ireland; official UK/Ireland sources may describe it as a national symbol rather than a formally designated “flower.” [S53, “Shamrock”] |
| 9.2 Waterloo was last Britain-France battle | **Corrected (false/overbroad)** | Waterloo (1815) was Napoleon’s final defeat, not the last battle involving Britain and France; the countries later fought together and against each other in other conflicts. [S54, “Battle of Waterloo”] |
| 9.3 £100 note is highest value in circulation: false | **Corrected (ambiguous)** | Bank of England’s highest denomination is £50, but Scottish and Northern Irish banks issue £100 notes. Thus false if specifically “Bank of England note”; not false as an all-UK-currency claim. [S55, “Current banknotes”; S56, “Banknotes in circulation”] |
| 9.4 Lake District is in England | Confirmed | Lake District National Park is in Cumbria, England. [S57, “About the Lake District”] |
| 9.5 Responsibilities: care for self/family; respect others’ rights/opinions | Confirmed, with wording nuance | GOV.UK citizenship pledge/values include respecting others’ rights and freedoms; self/family care is a civic responsibility rather than a specific general legal duty. [S1] |
| 9.6 Shakespeare born Stratford-upon-Avon | Confirmed | Shakespeare was born in Stratford-upon-Avon, according to Shakespeare Birthplace Trust (public heritage institution). [S58, “Birthplace”] |
| 9.7 Women could vote at 21 same as men in 1928 | Confirmed, with legal wording | Representation of the People (Equal Franchise) Act 1928 equalised women’s voting age at 21; later lowered to 18 in 1969. [S45, Act title and provisions] |
| 9.8 Hogmanay is Scottish New Year’s Eve | Confirmed | National Records of Scotland describes Hogmanay as the Scottish New Year celebration. [S59, “Hogmanay”] |
| 9.9 Northern Ireland capital Belfast | Confirmed | Northern Ireland Statistics and Research Agency / NI government sources identify Belfast as the capital. [S60, “Northern Ireland facts”] |
| 9.10 Boer War 1899–1902 in South Africa | Confirmed, shorthand | Second Boer War ran 1899–1902 in southern Africa. [S61, “Boer War”] |
| 9.11 Individual voter registration requires each person’s own application | Confirmed, qualification | Individual Electoral Registration means each person is responsible for their own registration; some people can be registered by an authorised person or through special arrangements. [S62, “Register to vote”] |
| 9.12 Devolved administrations do not control defence/immigration | Confirmed | Defence and immigration are reserved to the UK Parliament/Government in the devolution settlements. [S10, reserved matters] |
| 9.13 UK/Irish/Commonwealth citizen minimum age 18 for public office | **Corrected (overbroad)** | Age 18 and citizenship requirements apply to candidacy for the House of Commons (with eligibility rules); “public office” broadly includes many roles with different rules and is not a single universal rule. [S63, “Who can stand”] |
| 9.14 Victorian free trade through abolishing import taxes | Confirmed, qualified | Repeal of the Corn Laws (1846) and Navigation Acts (1849) advanced free trade; import duties were not abolished generally. [S64, “Repeal of the Corn Laws”; S65] |
| 9.15 Nelson commanded British fleet at Trafalgar | Confirmed | Nelson commanded the British fleet at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805. [S66, “Battle of Trafalgar”] |
| 9.16 Serfs were peasants working a lord’s agricultural land | Confirmed, broad | Serfdom involved peasants bound to a lord/estate and obligations to work land; exact status varied by place and period. [S67, “Serfdom”] |
| 9.17 Steam power drove industrial development | Confirmed, broad | Steam engines were a major driver of industrialisation, alongside other technologies, capital and labour changes. [S68, “Industrial Revolution”] |
| 9.18 MPs can only be contacted by post | **Corrected (false)** | Parliament provides email/contact details and constituency office routes as well as postal addresses. [S41, contact options] |
| 9.19 Reformation challenged Pope | Confirmed, shorthand | As 7.19: English Reformation broke with papal authority; broader Protestant Reformation challenged papal authority across Europe. [S20] |
| 9.20 William the Conqueror introduced Domesday Book | Confirmed, qualified | William ordered the survey in 1085; the completed record is known as Domesday Book. “Introduced” is imprecise. [S69, “Domesday Book”] |
| 9.21 Butler Act 1944 free secondary education England/Wales | Confirmed, qualified | Education Act 1944 reorganised English and Welsh education and provided for free secondary education; later legislation changed the system. [S70, “Education Act 1944”] |
| 9.22 Small claims is informal, cheap route for minor disputes | Confirmed, jurisdiction-limited | In England and Wales the small claims track is intended for lower-value, simpler civil claims and limits recoverable legal costs; Scotland and NI have different procedures. [S71, “Making a court claim”] |
| 9.23 Crystal Palace built in Hyde Park for 1851 Exhibition | Confirmed | Built in Hyde Park for the Great Exhibition of 1851. [S72, “Crystal Palace”] |
| 9.24 Florence Nightingale was a nurse | Confirmed | Florence Nightingale was a nurse and social reformer. [S73, “Florence Nightingale”] |
