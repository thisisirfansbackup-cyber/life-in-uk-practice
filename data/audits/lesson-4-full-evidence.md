## Notes on the seven items needing care

- **73** — Keep the answer but avoid asserting a quoted “last battle” fact. Better: “Which 1815 battle ended Napoleon's wars with Britain?” The NAM page supports the battle, the date, and that Britain and France were allies thereafter.
- **74** — Single-answer framing works only because the leek is a vegetable/emblem, not a “flower”. Say “national flower” explicitly in the stem.
- **76** — Replace “Every five years” with “At least every five years” or restate as maximum parliamentary term. Otherwise the item teaches a fixed cycle that the 2011–2022 statute book briefly created and then removed.
- **77** — The strongest correction in this set. Official position: Scottish notes are legal currency, fully backed, but not legal tender anywhere in the UK, and a shop may refuse them. A True/False “valid everywhere” item cannot be answered cleanly.
- **86** — “Poems” is defensible but loose; the Tales are a verse frame-narrative. Consider “a collection of stories in verse”.
- **91** — “Racial crime” should read “racially aggravated offences”; the smoking offence is created by Health Act 2006 s 7 (England), with equivalent smoke-free provisions in the devolved nations.
- **96** — “Equal time” is a US-style phrase; UK broadcast regulation uses “due impartiality” and “due weight”. The answer set (TV and radio) is correct, and newspapers/internet are not covered by these rules.
