## Findings

- Answers 193–216 are supported, with corrupted options replaced and four distinct choices.
- **193:** the shamrock is the conventional flower associated with Northern Ireland. The handbook has no floral-emblems section, so the source is CAIN (Ulster University), which describes the shamrock as one of Ireland's national emblems used in Northern Ireland and on St Patrick's Day. This is an academic archive rather than a government statement; no stronger official page was accessible.
- **194:** the claim is framed as the French Wars ending with Napoleon's defeat at Waterloo in 1815, which the handbook supports, rather than the overbroad “last battle between Britain and France”.
- **195:** the original “£100 is the highest note” is ambiguous because Scottish and Northern Irish banks issue £100 notes. The runtime true/false statement asks about the highest-value **Bank of England** note, which is £50, so the statement is false.
- **197:** restored as a genuine select-two: looking after yourself and your family; respecting others' rights.
- **204:** restored as a genuine select-two: defence and immigration are not devolved.
- **205:** “most citizens” keeps the handbook's exceptions.
- **213:** the Butler Act introduced free secondary education in England and Wales.
- No unresolved factual blocker in this lesson; the shamrock source is the weakest citation in the set and is documented above.
